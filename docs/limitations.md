# Limitations, interpretation and ethical use

1. **Not an adjudication tool.** Source PotentialFraud is a provider dataset label; claim anomalies and rule signals are not verified individual fraud. No automatic denial, punishment or clinical decision should follow.
2. **Probabilities are uncalibrated.** Provider predict_proba is shown with that warning. IF/LOF scores have their own units and thresholds, not percentage fraud likelihoods. Hybrid results keep all outputs separate.
3. **Retrospective evidence.** Random provider splitting estimates generalization to held-out providers in this dataset, not future claims or another payer/country. Source dates lack claim arrival/adjudication times. Earlier start dates do not guarantee operational availability at scoring time. Duplicate/repeated-procedure rules are retrospective.
4. **Holdout scope.** The holdout was inspected during iterative development. It was not used to choose the winner (training CV AP was), but final results are internal evaluation, not untouched external validation. An independent dataset/time holdout is needed for stronger claims.
5. **No claim fraud metrics.** No precision/recall/ROC-AUC for claim anomalies is claimed. Some source claims used in anomaly fitting are also scored; those scores are descriptive and are not novelty evaluation. LOF in-sample novelty scoring is not equivalent to its fit-time outlier factor. Clinical reviewer adjudication is needed to evaluate usefulness.
6. **Heuristic thresholds.** Anomaly contamination 5%, >5x historical mean, >50 prior patient claims, robust deviation >50 and pair frequency <=1 are transparent research defaults, not clinically optimized thresholds. Union-of-indicators increases review volume. High costs and rare procedures can be entirely legitimate.
7. **Clinical codes.** Rare pair uses primary diagnosis/first procedure only, not a medically validated diagnosis/procedure compatibility engine. Procedure/diagnosis slot absence is not intrinsically suspicious.
8. **Physician analytics.** Counts and amounts are descriptive, across all recorded physician roles; no physician fraud classifier exists. No names or specialty attributes are invented.
9. **Local resource limits.** Whole-table preparation/training is not constant memory. Recommended 8 GB RAM; low-RAM Windows benchmarking remains unverified. No disk-partitioned preprocessing or async export jobs. CSV/PDF exports are capped and PDFs use compact tabular formatting.
10. **Security/privacy.** JWT role protection is implemented, but no TLS, MFA, account recovery, brute-force rate limiting, revocation list, field-level de-identification, regulatory compliance certification or production hardening. Keep localhost-only, source data and exports private. Joblib is executable pickle: load only trusted local artifacts. Session tokens live in sessionStorage; avoid running untrusted scripts in the same origin.
11. **Database lifecycle.** Current-score snapshots are stored, not longitudinal model history. Refresh resets review statuses. New datasets need a fresh database and full ingestion. Model IDs have a research version convention; hashes in metrics identify exact sources.
12. **Scope of testing.** Successfully exercised on Linux with real PostgreSQL and Chromium. Windows commands are provided but have not been executed on the user's laptop. No claim of Windows certification or exhaustive testing.

## Sensible extensions
Independent temporal/external validation; nested CV and calibrated provider probabilities; expert-reviewed anomaly labels; clinical code reference vocabularies; fair-use/fairness auditing; incremental history stores; streaming preprocessing; independently versioned immutable models; robust account controls and comprehensive privacy review.
